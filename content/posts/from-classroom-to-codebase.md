Before I built platforms, I stood in front of classrooms. In 2021 I worked as a teaching assistant at Kirindine Secondary School, and in 2023 I taught Mathematics and Physics at Thaicu Secondary School. Both placements were in Meru County, and both shaped how I work today.

## Explaining things simply

A teacher cannot hide behind jargon. If a student does not understand, the explanation failed, not the student. I carry that into software. When I write documentation, design a form or explain an architecture to a client, I ask the same question: would this make sense to someone seeing it for the first time?

## Planning before performing

Drafting lesson plans taught me to start with the outcome and work backwards. Every sprint, every feature and every client project I run now begins the same way: what should be true at the end, and what are the steps to get there?

## Patience with mistakes

Students learn by getting things wrong, and so do developers. Debugging is just teaching yourself, one failed assumption at a time.

## Leadership through mentoring

Mentoring students gave me my first experience of leading people. It is the reason mentorship sits at the heart of IsoMount Tech Collective. I left the classroom, but I never stopped teaching.
